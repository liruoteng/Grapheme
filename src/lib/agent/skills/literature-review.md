# Literature Review Guide

## Purpose
A literature review synthesizes existing research to establish context, identify gaps, and justify your research contribution.

## Search Strategy

### Database Selection
- **Semantic Scholar**: Broad coverage, citation metrics
- **Google Scholar**: Comprehensive, includes grey literature
- **IEEE Xplore**: Engineering and computer science
- **PubMed**: Biomedical and life sciences
- **ACM Digital Library**: Computing research

### Search Techniques
1. Start with key terms from your research question
2. Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT)
3. Check references of relevant papers (backward chaining)
4. Find papers that cite key works (forward chaining)
5. Filter by year, citation count, and venue quality

## Writing Structure

### Thematic Organization
Group papers by theme, not chronologically:
```
1. Theme A: [papers discussing concept X]
2. Theme B: [papers on methodology Y]
3. Theme C: [papers addressing problem Z]
4. Gap: What's missing from existing work
```

### For Each Paper
- **Cite**: Use @bibKey format
- **Context**: Why is this paper relevant?
- **Contribution**: What did they find/propose?
- **Limitation**: What didn't they address?

### Synthesis Phrases
- "Several studies have shown..." @ref1 @ref2 @ref3
- "In contrast to @ref1, @ref2 found..."
- "Building on the work of @ref1..."
- "A gap remains in understanding..."

## Quality Checklist
- [ ] Covers seminal works in the field
- [ ] Includes recent publications (last 3-5 years)
- [ ] Identifies clear research gap
- [ ] Synthesizes rather than lists papers
- [ ] Connects literature to your research questions
