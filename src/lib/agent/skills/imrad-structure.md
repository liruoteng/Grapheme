# IMRaD Paper Structure

The IMRaD format (Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion) is the standard structure for empirical research papers.

## Required Sections

### 1. Introduction
- Establish the research problem and its significance
- Review relevant literature to identify the gap
- State research questions or hypotheses
- Preview the approach and key findings

**Length**: Typically 10-15% of total paper

### 2. Methods
- Describe participants/subjects and sampling
- Detail materials, instruments, or data sources
- Explain procedures step-by-step
- Specify data analysis methods
- Include enough detail for replication

**Length**: Typically 15-20% of total paper

### 3. Results
- Present findings without interpretation
- Use tables and figures for complex data
- Report statistical analyses with effect sizes
- Address each research question/hypothesis
- Include both significant and non-significant findings

**Length**: Typically 20-25% of total paper

### 4. Discussion
- Interpret findings in context of literature
- Explain unexpected results
- Discuss theoretical and practical implications
- Acknowledge limitations
- Suggest future research directions

**Length**: Typically 25-30% of total paper

## Additional Sections

### Abstract
- Structured: Background, Methods, Results, Conclusions
- 150-300 words (check journal requirements)
- Include key findings and implications

### Conclusion
- Summarize main contributions
- Restate significance
- Final takeaway message

### References
- Follow citation style consistently
- Include all cited works
- Prefer recent, peer-reviewed sources
