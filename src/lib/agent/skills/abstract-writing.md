# Abstract Writing Guide

## Structure (Structured Abstract)

### Background/Purpose (1-2 sentences)
- What problem does this research address?
- Why is it important?

### Methods (1-2 sentences)
- What approach did you take?
- What data/materials were used?

### Results (2-3 sentences)
- What were the key findings?
- Include specific numbers when possible

### Conclusions (1-2 sentences)
- What do the results mean?
- What are the implications?

## Word Count Guidelines
- Most journals: 150-300 words
- Conference papers: 100-250 words
- Always check target venue requirements

## Writing Tips

### Do
- Write the abstract last, after the paper is complete
- Use active voice: "We analyzed..." not "An analysis was performed..."
- Include key terms for discoverability
- Make it self-contained (no references to figures/tables)

### Don't
- Include citations
- Use abbreviations without definition
- Reference figures or tables
- Include information not in the paper
- Exceed the word limit

## Example Template
```
[Problem statement and significance]. [Research objective].
[Methods overview]. [Key finding 1 with data].
[Key finding 2 with data]. [Main conclusion and implication].
```

## Keywords
After the abstract, include 4-6 keywords that:
- Reflect the main topics
- Include terms researchers would search for
- Avoid overly general terms
